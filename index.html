Make Arabic, Persian, Urdu and Hebrew text display correctly in apps without RTL support

RTL Text Fixer is a Windows 10/11 app. Many video, motion-graphics, 3D, game-engine and design tools draw Arabic as separate letters in reverse order and show Hebrew backwards. Paste your text into RTL Text Fixer and copy the result — the letters are connected (Unicode presentation forms) and the text is put into visual left-to-right order, so the app shows it the way it should read. Or copy text anywhere, press Ctrl+Shift+R and paste.

How it works

  1. Paste or type your text in the left box. The script and language are detected automatically.
  2. The preview on the right shows exactly how the result will look in an app without RTL support. Click Copy result and paste it into your project.
  3. Without opening the window: copy text in any app, press Ctrl+Shift+R (configurable) and paste. Press the hotkey again to get the original text back.

Supported languages and scripts

Arabic-script languages (letters are connected and reordered): Arabic, Persian (Farsi), Urdu, Kurdish (Sorani), Pashto, Sindhi, Uyghur — all 76 letters that have connected forms in Unicode, including پ چ ژ گ ک ی, ٹ ڈ ڑ ں ہ ھ ے and ۆ ۇ ۈ ۋ ې, plus the lam-alef ligatures لا لأ لإ لآ. Hebrew and Yiddish (reordered; Hebrew needs no shaping), with niqqud kept or removed. Numbers and English words inside the text keep the right order (Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm).

Features

  • Connected Arabic-script letters (initial, medial, final and isolated forms) and lam-alef ligatures
  • Visual left-to-right order with numbers, Latin words and punctuation in the right place; brackets mirrored
  • Automatic script and language detection; warns if the text is already reshaped
  • Diacritics (harakat, niqqud): keep, remove, or spacing forms for apps that cannot place marks
  • Multi-line text, optional reverse line order and word wrap at a chosen length
  • “Unshape” turns reshaped text back into normal editable text
  • Global clipboard hotkey (Ctrl+Shift+R) and tray icon; optional start with Windows
  • Interface in 12 languages: English, Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Hebrew, Turkish, Russian, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese (Brazil), Hindi — right-to-left layout for Arabic, Persian, Urdu and Hebrew
  • Works offline: no internet access, no account, no ads, no telemetry

Limitations

  • Windows 10 (1809 or later) and Windows 11 only.
  • Letters that have no connected forms in Unicode cannot be joined by any reshaper — e.g. Kurdish ڵ ڕ ێ and Pashto ټ ځ څ ډ ړ ږ ښ ګ ڼ ۍ. They are shown unconnected (Kurdish ە is drawn with the identical heh shapes).
  • The font used in your app must contain Arabic presentation forms (Arial, Tahoma, Segoe UI and Noto Sans Arabic do). Urdu appears in Naskh style, not Nastaliq.
  • The result is meant for display. If the app re-wraps a long line, the lines come out in the wrong order — use the wrap option or break lines yourself.
  • It reshapes, it does not translate.

Frequently asked questions

Why are Arabic letters disconnected and reversed in Premiere Pro, and how do I fix it?

Programs that render text without Arabic shaping and bidirectional support draw every letter in its isolated form from left to right. Recent Premiere Pro versions have a Middle Eastern text engine you can enable in the Type preferences; if your version, template or plugin does not use it, paste the text into RTL Text Fixer (or copy it and press Ctrl+Shift+R) and paste the result into your title.

How do I fix Persian text that appears reversed in After Effects?

Enable the Middle Eastern and South Asian text engine if your After Effects version has it. Otherwise convert the text with RTL Text Fixer: Persian letters such as پ چ ژ گ ک ی are connected, the half-space (ZWNJ) is respected, and the words are put in visual order so the layer shows the text correctly.

Hebrew text shows up backwards in Unity. How do I fix it?

Unity’s standard text components do not reorder right-to-left text. RTL Text Fixer reorders Hebrew (and Yiddish) into visual order while keeping numbers and English words readable, so you can paste the result into a Text or TextMeshPro field. For Arabic, Persian and Urdu it also connects the letters.

How can I write Arabic or Urdu in Blender?

Blender’s 3D text objects do not shape Arabic-script text. Convert the text with RTL Text Fixer and paste it into the text object, and choose a font that contains Arabic presentation forms (for example Noto Sans Arabic). For long text, use the wrap option so each line is reordered on its own.

What is an Arabic reshaper?

A reshaper replaces each Arabic letter with the Unicode presentation form that matches its position in the word (initial, medial, final or isolated) and converts the text to visual order. Apps without Arabic support can then draw the text correctly, because they only need to place the pre-shaped glyphs from left to right.

Do numbers and English words stay in the right order?

Yes. RTL Text Fixer implements the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm, so numbers like 2025 or 1,250.75, English words, punctuation and brackets inside right-to-left text keep their correct order and position.

Why do some Kurdish or Pashto letters stay unconnected?

Unicode has no connected presentation forms for letters such as Kurdish ڵ ڕ ێ or Pashto ټ ډ ړ ښ ګ ڼ. A presentation-form reshaper can therefore not join them; RTL Text Fixer keeps them as they are and draws their neighbours unconnected on that side so nothing looks half-joined.

Can I turn reshaped text back into normal text?

Yes. Switch to Unshape mode, or copy reshaped text and press the hotkey — RTL Text Fixer converts the presentation forms back to normal letters in logical order. Pressing the hotkey right after a conversion restores the exact original text.

Does RTL Text Fixer send my text anywhere?

No. It works completely offline, has no telemetry or analytics, and does not save the text you convert.
